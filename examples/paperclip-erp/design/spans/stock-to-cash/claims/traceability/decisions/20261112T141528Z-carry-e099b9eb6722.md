  # Finished cartons and quality
  
  Stock is a set of identified cartons, not just a number on a dashboard. A carton is on hand until the carrier collects it. It can be free, reserved for one order, or picked for that order. Picking moves it to the packing area; it remains on hand and reserved. Collected cartons remain in the traceability record.
Cites design/brief/stock.md#holds-after-release:
  ## Holds after release
  
  QA can put a released batch back on hold, even after a carton is reserved, picked or collected. A hold immediately blocks new reservations and collection of the affected cartons still on hand. It does not erase existing reservations: those orders wait for release or the customer cancels the uncollected remainder. QA can release the batch again.
  
  Each QA decision has its own operation identity and names the batch's current quality revision. A successful hold or release increments that revision. A new operation with a stale revision is refused. Replaying a successful operation returns its recorded result without changing the current quality state, even if newer decisions have intervened. Releasing a batch after a new hold therefore requires a fresh QA decision; an old release message cannot lift the hold.
  
  For cartons collected before a later hold, staff can find every affected shipment and customer from the retained batch links. We examine the completeness of those links, not whether support contacts customers or retrieves goods. A later hold cannot make an earlier authorised collection unhappen.
Cites design/brief/overview.md#the-business-we-examine:
  ## The business we examine
  
  There is one warehouse beside the production line, one paperclip product and a fixed price of £10 per carton. All customers in this slice buy on account. An order has one customer and a positive whole number of cartons. It can leave in several shipments. Every carton has a unique identity and belongs to one batch; every shipment belongs to one order. The same rules apply when there are several customers, orders and batches.
  
  The software records a completed batch at the warehouse door. Wire purchasing, line scheduling and work in progress are outside this slice. The end is an invoice settled by a recorded bank receipt. Card capture, refunds, tax, credit notes, bad debt, accounting export, customer login and delivery tracking are outside it too. This lets a reader follow one complete stock-to-cash story without treating the entire business as one model.

  ## Northline buys, then pays
  
  QA releases a finished batch. Northline Office has a £100 credit limit and orders four cartons. Confirmation reserves four cartons and commits £40. Two cartons leave in the first shipment and two in the second. Finance issues a £20 invoice for each shipment. Northline pays the first invoice in two £10 receipts and the second in one £20 receipt. Exposure ends at zero, all four cartons are collected, and every invoice leads back through its shipment to its order, customer and production batch. Repeated confirmation, collection, invoice issue and receipt application must leave the totals unchanged.
