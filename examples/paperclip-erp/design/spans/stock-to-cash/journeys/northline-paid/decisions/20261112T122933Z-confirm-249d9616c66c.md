  ## Northline buys, then pays
  
  QA releases a finished batch. Northline Office has a £100 credit limit and orders four cartons. Confirmation reserves four cartons and commits £40. Two cartons leave in the first shipment and two in the second. Finance issues a £20 invoice for each shipment. Northline pays the first invoice in two £10 receipts and the second in one £20 receipt. Exposure ends at zero, all four cartons are collected, and every invoice leads back through its shipment to its order, customer and production batch. Repeated confirmation, collection, invoice issue and receipt application must leave the totals unchanged.

  ## The boundary of the result
  
  The application keeps the records in one authoritative store, with serializable isolation. Each named business operation commits all its effects together or has no effect; concurrent operations must behave as some serial order of those commits. A stale screen is never authority to confirm an order, change a batch's quality decision or dispatch a carton. Implementing that transaction guarantee and recovering the database from storage failure are outside this examination, so the result depends on that guarantee.
  
  Staff commands carry stable business identities. A repeated command refers to the same order, batch, shipment, invoice or bank receipt; it cannot mean a new one. Successful effects are remembered. A refusal makes no business change and does not consume an identity: the same request may be retried after the reason for refusal is resolved. Reusing a successful identity with different contents is refused. Software validates each command against current records. We assume scans identify the actual physical cartons, staff record bank receipts truthfully and the carrier takes exactly the cartons in an authorised collection. The software cannot establish these physical facts. It also cannot guarantee that staff, customers or the carrier eventually act. This slice makes safety claims and demonstrates possible journeys, not a promise that every order eventually ships.

  ## Picking and preparing a shipment
  
  A picker gathers scans, then submits one preparation command with a new shipment identity, its order and its complete non-empty set of cartons. Scan gathering before submission changes no stock records. Preparation checks all cartons are reserved for the order, released and not already in another prepared shipment, then marks them picked together. Shipment membership is fixed after that. An exact replay adds nothing; reusing its identity with different contents is refused, even after collection or cancellation. There may be more than one prepared shipment for an order. A shipment is prepared, collected or void; collected and void shipments cannot be edited. Packing and printing a label do not count as collection or create an invoice.
Cites design/brief/accounts.md#exposure-before-and-after-collection:
  ## Exposure before and after collection
  
  The customer's exposure is the value of confirmed, uncollected cartons plus the value of collected shipments not yet invoiced plus the unpaid balance of issued invoices. Each value belongs in exactly one of those buckets. Confirmation increases exposure; cancellation of the remainder reduces it. Collection and invoice issue only move value between buckets. They never open temporary credit headroom. Exposure never exceeds the customer's limit.
Cites design/brief/accounts.md#recording-payment:
  ## Recording payment
  
  Finance applies a bank receipt to one issued invoice. A receipt has a unique reference and a positive amount no greater than that invoice's remaining balance. Partial payments are allowed. Applying it reduces the invoice balance and the customer's exposure together. Replaying the same receipt has no further effect; trying its reference with a different invoice or amount is refused. Overpayment and payment before invoice issue are refused without changing credit or balances or consuming the receipt reference; a later valid retry can succeed. We assume finance matches the actual bank receipt correctly; reconciliation with the bank is outside this examination.
