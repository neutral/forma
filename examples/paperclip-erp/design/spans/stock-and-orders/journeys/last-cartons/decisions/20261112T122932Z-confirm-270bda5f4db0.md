  ## The last cartons and the last credit
  
  Northline and Pinpoint Print both ask for the last two available cartons. The first successful confirmation gets them; the other leaves no partial reservation or credit commitment. A separate case has enough cartons but two Northline orders competing for the last £20 of credit. Only one is confirmed. This remains true after the first order ships while finance has not yet issued its invoice. Paying a valid invoice frees credit for a later order.

  ## Confirming an order
  
  Confirmation succeeds only when enough released cartons are available and the customer has credit for the whole order. It reserves particular cartons and commits the full order value against that customer's credit in the same operation. If either check fails, neither stock nor credit changes. Two orders competing for the last cartons or the last credit cannot both take them. After a successful confirmation, confirming the same order again returns its recorded result and adds nothing. A refusal for stock or credit leaves the draft retryable when circumstances change.

  ## The boundary of the result
  
  The application keeps the records in one authoritative store, with serializable isolation. Each named business operation commits all its effects together or has no effect; concurrent operations must behave as some serial order of those commits. A stale screen is never authority to confirm an order, change a batch's quality decision or dispatch a carton. Implementing that transaction guarantee and recovering the database from storage failure are outside this examination, so the result depends on that guarantee.
  
  Staff commands carry stable business identities. A repeated command refers to the same order, batch, shipment, invoice or bank receipt; it cannot mean a new one. Successful effects are remembered. A refusal makes no business change and does not consume an identity: the same request may be retried after the reason for refusal is resolved. Reusing a successful identity with different contents is refused. Software validates each command against current records. We assume scans identify the actual physical cartons, staff record bank receipts truthfully and the carrier takes exactly the cartons in an authorised collection. The software cannot establish these physical facts. It also cannot guarantee that staff, customers or the carrier eventually act. This slice makes safety claims and demonstrates possible journeys, not a promise that every order eventually ships.
