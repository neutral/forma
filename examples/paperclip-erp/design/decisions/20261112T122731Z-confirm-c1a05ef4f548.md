  Coil & Clip makes paperclips and sells sealed cartons to office suppliers. This project examines one connected slice of its ERP: recording finished stock, reserving it for orders, stopping unsafe dispatches, and keeping the customer's credit exposure correct through collection, invoicing and payment. A warehouse picker, a sales representative, quality assurance and finance all work on the same facts. Their actions can overlap.

  ## The business we examine
  
  There is one warehouse beside the production line, one paperclip product and a fixed price of £10 per carton. All customers in this slice buy on account. An order has one customer and a positive whole number of cartons. It can leave in several shipments. Every carton has a unique identity and belongs to one batch; every shipment belongs to one order. The same rules apply when there are several customers, orders and batches.
  
  The software records a completed batch at the warehouse door. Wire purchasing, line scheduling and work in progress are outside this slice. The end is an invoice settled by a recorded bank receipt. Card capture, refunds, tax, credit notes, bad debt, accounting export, customer login and delivery tracking are outside it too. This lets a reader follow one complete stock-to-cash story without treating the entire business as one model.

  Stock is a set of identified cartons, not just a number on a dashboard. A carton is on hand until the carrier collects it. It can be free, reserved for one order, or picked for that order. Picking moves it to the packing area; it remains on hand and reserved. Collected cartons remain in the traceability record.
Cites design/brief/stock.md#recording-a-batch:
  ## Recording a batch
  
  A finished batch enters once, with its identities and quantity checked, and starts on quality hold. Repeating that batch receipt does not create more cartons. QA releases the batch after its inspection. Only released cartons can be reserved. Available means released, on hand and unreserved. We do not correct counts, scrap batches, transfer stock or substitute products in this slice.
Cites design/brief/stock.md#holds-after-release:
  ## Holds after release
  
  QA can put a released batch back on hold, even after a carton is reserved, picked or collected. A hold immediately blocks new reservations and collection of the affected cartons still on hand. It does not erase existing reservations: those orders wait for release or the customer cancels the uncollected remainder. QA can release the batch again.
  
  Each QA decision has its own operation identity and names the batch's current quality revision. A successful hold or release increments that revision. A new operation with a stale revision is refused. Replaying a successful operation returns its recorded result without changing the current quality state, even if newer decisions have intervened. Releasing a batch after a new hold therefore requires a fresh QA decision; an old release message cannot lift the hold.
  
  For cartons collected before a later hold, staff can find every affected shipment and customer from the retained batch links. We examine the completeness of those links, not whether support contacts customers or retrieves goods. A later hold cannot make an earlier authorised collection unhappen.

Cites design/brief/orders.md#confirming-an-order:
  ## Confirming an order
  
  Confirmation succeeds only when enough released cartons are available and the customer has credit for the whole order. It reserves particular cartons and commits the full order value against that customer's credit in the same operation. If either check fails, neither stock nor credit changes. Two orders competing for the last cartons or the last credit cannot both take them. After a successful confirmation, confirming the same order again returns its recorded result and adds nothing. A refusal for stock or credit leaves the draft retryable when circumstances change.
Cites design/brief/orders.md#picking-and-preparing-a-shipment:
  ## Picking and preparing a shipment
  
  A picker gathers scans, then submits one preparation command with a new shipment identity, its order and its complete non-empty set of cartons. Scan gathering before submission changes no stock records. Preparation checks all cartons are reserved for the order, released and not already in another prepared shipment, then marks them picked together. Shipment membership is fixed after that. An exact replay adds nothing; reusing its identity with different contents is refused, even after collection or cancellation. There may be more than one prepared shipment for an order. A shipment is prepared, collected or void; collected and void shipments cannot be edited. Packing and printing a label do not count as collection or create an invoice.
Cites design/brief/orders.md#authorising-collection:
  ## Authorising collection
  
  At the carrier handoff, the application checks the order is still open, every carton in the shipment is still reserved for it, and every batch is currently released. It then records collection, removes those cartons from on-hand stock and reservation, and creates a durable obligation to invoice that shipment, all together. The authorisation and the physical handoff are one business event at the boundary described in the overview. A hold or cancellation committed before it wins; one committed afterwards does not undo the collection.
  
  Repeated collection of the same shipment adds no movement or obligation. A partially collected order remains open for its remaining cartons; it completes when all its cartons have been collected. The system never collects a carton twice, a held carton, or the cancelled remainder of an order. A refusal changes none of these records.
Cites design/brief/orders.md#cancelling-the-remainder:
  ## Cancelling the remainder
  
  The customer may cancel any uncollected remainder of a confirmed open order, including picked cartons and prepared shipments, in one operation. Cancellation releases its reservations and credit commitment and voids its uncollected shipments. The cartons return to free stock, but a held carton stays unavailable. Already collected shipments, their invoice obligations and their invoices remain. An order with a cancelled remainder accepts no further picking or collection. Repeating cancellation does nothing further. A draft has no committed remainder to cancel, and a fully collected order cannot be cancelled here. Discarding a draft is outside this slice.

Cites design/brief/accounts.md#exposure-before-and-after-collection:
  ## Exposure before and after collection
  
  The customer's exposure is the value of confirmed, uncollected cartons plus the value of collected shipments not yet invoiced plus the unpaid balance of issued invoices. Each value belongs in exactly one of those buckets. Confirmation increases exposure; cancellation of the remainder reduces it. Collection and invoice issue only move value between buckets. They never open temporary credit headroom. Exposure never exceeds the customer's limit.
Cites design/brief/accounts.md#issuing-invoices:
  ## Issuing invoices
  
  Finance issues one invoice for each collected shipment from its durable obligation, using that shipment's identity. The invoice has the shipment's customer and collected quantity times £10. An uncollected shipment has no invoice. Repeating invoice issue returns the same invoice. A delayed invoice job must not allow the customer to spend the same credit twice. No obligation disappears until the corresponding invoice exists; cancelling an order does not remove it.
Cites design/brief/accounts.md#recording-payment:
  ## Recording payment
  
  Finance applies a bank receipt to one issued invoice. A receipt has a unique reference and a positive amount no greater than that invoice's remaining balance. Partial payments are allowed. Applying it reduces the invoice balance and the customer's exposure together. Replaying the same receipt has no further effect; trying its reference with a different invoice or amount is refused. Overpayment and payment before invoice issue are refused without changing credit or balances or consuming the receipt reference; a later valid retry can succeed. We assume finance matches the actual bank receipt correctly; reconciliation with the bank is outside this examination.
