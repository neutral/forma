    ## Trace
    
    The intended story creates the original receipt, loses its reply, then retries the same key and receives that original receipt while the ticket remains active. In the examined prefix the original reply instead reaches the recipient. The recipient has therefore observed a reply and cannot take the next step, sending a retry because no reply was received; the intended exchange never runs to completion.
    
    ## Failure shape
    
    Recovery after an uncertain reply is blocked because the required lost-reply outcome is absent from this story. That recovery is wanted, and its separate underlying hazard is a retry that is not idempotent: repeating creation instead of returning the original durable result. Specification refers to the designed exchange, with no deployed implementation being judged. A replacement receipt is unwanted.
    
    ## Mechanism
    
    The recorded TLC search stopped at four states and depth four, not after all six steps. `Init` sets the recipient to `up` and the original reply route to `direct`. The wrapper takes only the scheduled actions; no `DropReply` can intervene. Consequently `ReturnReceipt` at step 3 delivers `r-a`, leaving `recipient.replies["a"] = {"r-a"}` and `audit.lost = {}`. `RequestRetry` at step 4 requires no observed reply and is disabled. The first incorrectly represented outcome is step 3; the first unreachable prefix ends at step 4. There is no journey start table and therefore no separate start question to settle.
    
    Recommend `model wrong` for this recorded failure: represent the already-stated lost-reply alternative within the reply action so this journey can exercise it, preserving successful delivery and the durable receipt. Keep the retry's no-observed-reply guard and the exact original-receipt requirement. The restatement already permits loss; adding an unlisted world step to the schedule or weakening `End` would change the question. This path ruling keeps no new journey.
    
    Private runs reproduced the result: prefixes 1, 2 and 3 were reached, but 4 was not. Changing only the initial original-reply route for a to `dropped` allowed all six actions, at seven states and depth seven, while the unchanged `End` still failed. The final receipts were `{"r-a", "r-a-retry"}` and the recipient received only the replacement. This diagnostic changes the transport input, not the original evidence, and gives no standing.
    
    That remaining failure is a separate design defect: `RetryClaim` deliberately allocates a fresh receipt, exactly as the revised proposal says. Correcting the loss representation alone must leave that defect visible for a real-finding ruling; a model-wrong repair must not silently replace the proposed handler with idempotent replay. Binding its fresh-id parameter to the old receipt would merely disable it. No bounds were reduced and no weaker form applies to this path question.
