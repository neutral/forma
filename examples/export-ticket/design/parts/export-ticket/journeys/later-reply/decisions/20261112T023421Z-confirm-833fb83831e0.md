Cites design/brief/overview.md#claim-design:
  ## Claim design
  
  The service owns tickets, pending checks and receipts. A ticket begins active, unclaimed and without any pending check or receipt. Expiry status (active or expired) and whether the ticket has been claimed are separate state: claiming does not prevent later expiry. Two distinct request keys may be in flight for the same ticket.
  
  A Check operation is permitted while the ticket is active and has no receipt. It remembers that this request key passed. Check neither claims the ticket nor creates a receipt. Each request key may be checked and completed once in this version.
  
  A Complete operation is permitted when its request key has a remembered passing check. It creates a fresh receipt and marks the ticket claimed in one atomic store action. Complete consults the remembered check; it does not recheck the ticket's current phase. Returning the created receipt to the recipient is a later operation and may lose its reply. A failed reply does not undo the stored receipt. This version does not retry a request key.
  
  Expiry is an independent operation. It changes an active ticket to expired, including while checks are pending. It does not delete remembered checks, receipts or the export. Once expired, the ticket cannot become active again.
Cites design/brief/overview.md#required-behavior:
  ## Required behavior
  
  No new receipt may be created after the ticket expires. At most one receipt may ever be created for a ticket, even when two request keys overlap. A claim completed before expiry must remain possible; refusing every claim is not an acceptable design. Expiry and concurrent request keys must remain possible.
  
  Whether a recipient may fetch an already-created receipt after expiry is not yet decided. Put that question to the developer; do not treat creating a new receipt and reading the original receipt as the same action.
